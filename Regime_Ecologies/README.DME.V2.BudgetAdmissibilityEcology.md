## 1. Purpose

This document defines the Budget / Admissibility Ecology of DME V2.

Budget / Admissibility is a cross-cutting support regime.

It governs constrained transition across:

```text
Structure
Runtime
Ledger
Projection / Index / Rasterization
Distinction Ecology
Agency Ecology
Decision / Consequence Ecology
Retention
Proof
Authority / Reference
Feedback / Integration
```

It answers:

```text
What has capacity?
What has permission?
What must defer?
What must be limited?
What must be rejected?
What must be quarantined?
What must route to proof?
What must route to consent?
What must route to governance?
What may proceed now?
```

This document does not define a formal schema.

It defines the conceptual ecology of capacity, permission, constraint, routing, receipt, and budget modulation.

---

## 2. Core Thesis

```text
Budget / Admissibility Ecology is the governance of constrained transition:
what has capacity, what has permission, what must defer, what must be tested,
what must be routed, and what may proceed.
```

Compressed:

```text
Budget = how much movement can occur.
Admissibility = whether movement may proceed.
Permit = scoped allowance for a transition.
Constraint = boundary shaping what movement is possible, safe, lawful, or allowed.
Receipt = what remains accountable after consequential transition.
```

Core law:

```text
Available capacity is not permission.
Permission without budget does not guarantee movement.
```

Budget and admissibility jointly prevent runtime pressure from becoming uncontrolled consequence.

---

## 3. Why Budget / Admissibility Exists

DME contains many pressure sources:

```text
structural admission pressure
runtime attention pressure
projection salience
retention echo
proof tension
agentic request
intention pressure
decision pressure
commitment pressure
feedback rupture
authority pressure
reference pressure
```

Pressure alone cannot decide movement.

Without Budget / Admissibility, DME risks collapse such as:

```text
salience → action
attention → intention
intention → permission
projection visibility → authority
memory echo → truth
proof request → execution
agent request → tool call
decision pressure → consequence
```

Budget / Admissibility prevents these collapses by requiring every transition to pass through capacity and permission constraints.

```text

```



## 11. AdmissibilityResult

### Definition

```text
AdmissibilityResult =
declared transition-permission outcome for a candidate movement.
```

Canonical result set:

```text
permit
permit_limited
defer
reject
quarantine
release
route_to_projection
route_to_retention
route_to_proof
route_to_consent
route_to_governance
route_to_repair
route_to_revocation
route_to_user
```

Each result should preserve:

```text
reason
scope
budget state
constraint refs
authority ceiling
required receipts
expiry / review condition
re-entry condition
```

### Result Meanings

```text
permit = transition may proceed under declared scope.
permit_limited = transition may proceed only under narrowed scope, reduced consequence,
  reduced visibility, or added receipt/proof/feedback constraints.
defer = transition should wait due to insufficient budget, uncertainty, missing context, or timing.
reject = transition should not proceed under current conditions.
quarantine = transition/object should be isolated from normal re-entry or consequence until reviewed.
release = candidate should stop consuming active attention/budget.
route_to_projection = candidate must become addressable/readable before further movement.
route_to_retention = candidate should persist or be archived rather than immediately acted upon.
route_to_proof = candidate requires load testing before escalation.
route_to_consent = candidate affects agentic stake and requires consent relation evaluation.
route_to_governance = candidate requires higher-order review due to scope, authority, risk, conflict, or uncertainty.
route_to_repair = candidate indicates rupture or mismatch requiring repair route.
route_to_revocation = candidate indicates current access, authority, reference, retention, or permission should be reduced.
route_to_user = candidate requires human decision, clarification, approval, or boundary update.
```

## 15. Consent as Specialized Agentic Admissibility

Consent is not the same as general admissibility.

```text
Admissibility = general transition-permission field.

Consent = specialized agentic admissibility relation involving loci of agency,
stake, consequence, and revocation.
```

Consent is required when a transition materially affects an agentic locus.

Consent evaluation asks:

```text
Who or what is affected?
What stake exists?
What consequence is projected?
What uncertainty remains?
What authority ceiling applies?
What can be revoked?
What changes materially from the original projection?
```

Non-collapse:

```text
permit ≠ consent
attention ≠ consent
agreement ≠ consent
consent ≠ blanket execution
consent ≠ permanent authority
```

Core law:

```text
Consent is admissibility under agentic stake.
```

## 16. Authority as Consequence Capacity

Authority is not the same as budget or admissibility.

```text
Authority = scoped, revocable consequence capacity.
```

Budget asks:

```text
Is capacity available?
```

Admissibility asks:

```text
May the transition proceed?
```

Authority asks:

```text
May this locus/process affect consequence under this scope?
```

Non-collapse:

```text
budget ≠ authority
admissibility ≠ authority
permit ≠ authority
authority ≠ truth
authority ≠ permanence
```

Core law:

```text
A permitted transition may still be authority-limited.
```

## 17. Proof as Load Survival Gate

Proof is not admissibility, but admissibility may require proof.

```text
Proof = survival testing under declared load.
```

A transition may route to proof when:

```text
support is weak
projection fidelity is uncertain
decision consequence is high
authority escalation is requested
reference promotion is requested
retention re-entry is risky
feedback contradicts expectation
```

Non-collapse:

```text
proof_required ≠ proof_passed
proof_passed ≠ universal authority
proof ≠ permission by itself
```

Core law:

```text
Proof can support admissibility.
Proof does not replace admissibility.
```

## 23. Budget Modulation

Budgets change through feedback.

Budget may increase when:

```text
coherent consequence repeats under scope
proof survives declared load
repair succeeds
projection fidelity improves
trust evidence strengthens
authority review passes
reference proves useful without rupture
```

Budget may decrease when:

```text
feedback ruptures
provenance breaks
projection misleads
retention echo contaminates runtime
authority is exceeded
consent is invalidated
proof fails
repair fails
stake harm occurs
```

Budget may also decay with time, inactivity, or changing context.

### Non-Claims

```text
budget_increase ≠ authority_grant
budget_decrease ≠ invalidation
budget_decay ≠ deletion
```
