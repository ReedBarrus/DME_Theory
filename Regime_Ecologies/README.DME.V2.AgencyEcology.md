## 1. Purpose

This document defines the Agency Ecology of DME V2.

Agency Ecology is one of the two primary runtime coordinate regimes derived from Distinction Space.

It explains how distinction topology localizes around loci of:

```text
observation
attention
interpretation
projection access
expectation
belief
request
intention
consent
stake
action
consequence reception
accountability
trust evidence
authority constraint
```

Agency Ecology does not define agency as personhood, consciousness, ownership, sovereignty, or unrestricted autonomy.

It defines agency as a structured causal-locality regime over the configuration-attention continuum.

---

## 2. Core Thesis

```text
Agency Ecology is the geometry of how distinction topology localizes into loci of
attention, interpretation, intention, consent, action, consequence, and accountability.
```

Compressed:

```text
Distinction = what can matter.
Agency = who or what can attend, interpret, request, act, be affected, and remain accountable.
Decision = what can branch, execute, change, repair, or revoke.
```

Agency is the bridge between distinction and decision.

A distinction may become agentic when it is organized around a locus that can:

```text
observe
attend
interpret
request
intend
consent
act
receive consequence
remain accountable
```

Final core law:

```text
Agency localizes distinction dynamics into accountable causal loci.
```

Agency Ecology inherits from Distinction Ecology.



## 6.5 AttentionEnvelope

```text
AttentionEnvelope =
the bounded agentic-runtime field around what a locus is attending to,
preserving projection context, distinction support, provenance, salience,
budget, scope, uncertainty, belief influence, and transition eligibility.
```

AttentionEnvelope is the missing vehicle between agentic access and agentic pressure.

It answers:

```text
What is this agentic locus attending to?
Through what projection?
Under what scope?
With what salience?
With what provenance support?
With what uncertainty or loss?
With what attention budget?
With what belief or expectation influence?
With what possible transition pressure?
```

AttentionLocus defines where attention localizes.

AttentionEnvelope defines the bounded attention event/context that may route into belief, request, intention, proof demand, or decision pressure.

### Contents

An AttentionEnvelope may contain or reference:

```text
AgenticLocus
ObserverFrame
```

### Lifecycle Position

The agency lifecycle should be read as:

```text
AgenticCandidate
→ AgenticLocus
→ ObserverFrame
→ ProjectionAccessProfile
→ AttentionEnvelope
→ Expectation / Belief Support
→ Request / Intention Pressure
→ Consent / Stake Boundary
→ ActionParticipation
→ ConsequenceReception
→ AccountabilityRecord
→ TrustEvidence / AuthorityConstraint
→ Retention / Proof / Reference Route
```

### Why AttentionEnvelope Exists

Without AttentionEnvelope, agency can collapse from projection into intention too quickly:

```text

→ salience spikes
→ request forms
→ intention pressure forms
→ decision pressure forms
```

AttentionEnvelope prevents this collapse by preserving:

```text
this attention was partial
this projection omitted axes
this provenance was weak
this salience does not authorize action
this belief may be biasing interpretation
this attention has budget and release conditions
```

```text

DecisionEnvelope
```

Full vehicle chain:


## 6.8.X CommitmentEnvelope and GoalProjection

Goals and commitments are derived agentic-decision lifecycle objects.

They are not required for every decision.

They become useful when an agentic locus needs to preserve intention across time, sessions, decisions, feedback cycles, and consequence updates.

### GoalProjection

```text
GoalProjection =
a projected desired consequence-configuration curvature.
```

A goal is not merely a wish.

A goal defines a desired configuration or outcome that can shape attention, decision relevance, feedback interpretation, repair, and future routing.

GoalProjection answers:

```text
What configuration is desired?
What consequence state is being aimed toward?
What would count as progress?
What would count as rupture?
What constraints must be preserved?
```

### CommitmentEnvelope

```text
CommitmentEnvelope =
the bounded agentic-decision field around a GoalProjection, preserving agentic locus,
intention pressure, scope, budget, constraints, decision relevance, feedback conditions,
repair paths, revocation conditions, and provenance.
```

CommitmentEnvelope answers:

```text
Who or what is committed?
Toward what goal?
Under what scope?
With what budget?
With what constraints?
What decisions does this pressure?
What consequences are acceptable?
What feedback updates progress?
What repair path exists?
When should the commitment decay, revise, or revoke?
```

### Goal / Intention / Commitment Distinction

```text
Goal = projected desired consequence-configuration curvature.

Intention = budgeted write-side attention from an agentic locus into decision topology.

Commitment = retained, accountable, revocable binding of intention toward a goal across time.
```

A goal can exist without commitment.

An intention can be momentary.

A commitment persists and re-enters runtime.

### Lifecycle Position

```text
AttentionEnvelope
→ Expectation / Belief Support
→ Request / Intention Pressure
→ GoalProjection
→ CommitmentEnvelope
→ Consent / Stake Boundary
→ ActionParticipation
→ ConsequenceReception
→ AccountabilityRecord
```

CommitmentEnvelope may also route directly into decision topology when decision relevance is present.

```text
CommitmentEnvelope
+ DecisionRelevance
+ Budget / Admissibility
+ Authority / Consent constraints
→ DecisionPressure
```

### Why CommitmentEnvelope Exists

Without CommitmentEnvelope, long-term goals collapse into loose intention pressure.

CommitmentEnvelope preserves:

```text
this goal persists across time
this intention is bounded
this scope applies
this budget applies
this feedback updates the commitment
this repair path exists
this commitment can decay, revise, or revoke
```

CommitmentEnvelope lets agency support active decision making without allowing desire to become permission.

### Non-Collapse Laws

```text

commitment ≠ authority
commitment ≠ permission
```


## 19. Agency Metric Families

No formal metrics are defined yet.

However, DME should preserve the following metric families for future schemas.

### Locus Continuity

How coherently an agentic locus persists across observation, projection, request, action, feedback, and retention.

### Projection Access Fidelity

How accurately projection surfaces expose what the locus needs without overexposing or distorting.

### Attention Integrity

How well attention allocation follows support, scope, relevance, and budget rather than capture or noise.
