This document defines the relationship between:

```text
constraint
```

It exists because DME cannot treat authority as arbitrary permission, trust as a scalar, or recursion as unbounded processing.

```text
What may receive attention?
What may persist?
```

## 2. Core Thesis

DME is constraint-driven, but constraint alone is static.

Budget is constraint made dynamic.

Final compression:

```text
Constraint defines lawful boundary.
Budget defines available movement through that boundary.
Coherence capacitance defines how much recursive pressure a structure can hold.
Proof tests whether a structure deserves more budget.
Trust records scoped reliability under feedback.
Access grants scoped exposure and affordance.
Authority grants scoped consequence capacity.
Reference stabilizes authority as future guidance.
Canon governs durable reference.
Sovereignty is exercised budget autonomy through decision space under conservation.
```

Budget is the metabolic driver of recursion.

Without budget, recursion blows up, collapses, or drifts.

## 3. Constraint vs Budget

### 3.1 Constraint

A constraint defines lawful transition geometry.

Constraint answers:

```text
What boundaries exist?
What must be preserved?
What is not allowed?
What scope applies?
What conservation burden must be satisfied?
```

Examples:

```text
lineage must be preserved
uncertainty must be carried
projection cannot become source
execution cannot exceed authority
retention cannot imply proof
external action requires review
canon must be revocable
```

### 3.2 Budget

A budget defines available transition capacity under constraint.

Budget answers:

```text
How much may be sampled?
How much may be processed?
How much may be projected?
How much may be retained?
How much may be proof-loaded?
How much may be executed?
How much may affect another system?
```

### 3.3 Combined Law

```text
constraint + budget = lawful energetic geometry
```

Constraint shapes possibility.

Budget meters movement.

Together they define how recursion remains coherent.

It is a vector of capacities across regimes.

### 4.1 Attention Budget

```text
capacity to sample, focus, amplify, compare, or route active structures
```

### 4.2 Runtime Budget

```text
capacity to compute, simulate, perturb, compare, or maintain active states
```

### 4.3 Projection Budget

```text
capacity to expose, rasterize, tokenize, embed, render, or summarize structure
```

### 4.4 Token / Embedding Rasterization Budget

```text
capacity to create symbolic handles and coordinate lenses over projected topology
```

### 4.5 Retention Budget

```text
capacity to preserve traces, maintain echo eligibility, archive, compress, or decay
```

### 4.6 Proof Budget

```text
capacity to load, perturb, replay, reconstruct, challenge, or validate candidates
```

### 4.7 Execution Budget

```text
capacity to emit effect into a target regime
```

### 4.8 Governance Budget

```text
capacity for review, stake accounting, authority modulation, contestation, and revocation
```

### 4.9 Ledger Budget

```text
capacity to preserve causal records, receipts, summaries, signatures, and reconstruction paths
```

### 4.10 Access Budget

```text
capacity granted to actors, agents, tools, users, or loci to see, query, use, affect, or request system resources
```

## 6. Coherence Capacitance

### 6.1 Definition

```text
Coherence capacitance =
the amount of recursive attention, projection, retention, proof, execution,
and consequence pressure a structure can hold without losing lineage,
uncertainty, scope, reversibility, stake alignment, or field coherence.
```

A structure with high coherence capacitance can hold more pressure.

A structure with low coherence capacitance must remain local, weakly retained, narrowly projected, proof-routed, repaired, or released.

### 6.2 Coherence Capacitance Axes

```text
lineage stability
uncertainty containment
scope preservation
projection fidelity
failure repairability
reversibility
stake alignment
feedback quality
cross-regime stability
attention stability
retention stability
proof survival
side-effect containment
```

### 6.3 Coherence Capacitance Rule

```text
No transition may consume more budget than its coherence capacitance can support.
```

## 7. Resource-Regenerating Invariance

A structure earns future budget only when its recurrence regenerates coherence.

### 7.1 Definition

```text
Resource-regenerating invariance =
a pattern that preserves enough identity across transformation while improving or restoring
the system’s capacity for coherent attention, projection, retention, execution, repair, or governance.
```

It is not enough that a pattern repeats.

It must repeat while regenerating system capacity.

### 7.2 Budget Increase Conditions

A candidate may receive increased budget when it demonstrates:

```text
coherence gain
uncertainty reduction
failure repair
lineage preservation
scope preservation
reversibility preservation
stake alignment
projection fidelity
side-effect containment
non-extractive consequence
proof survival
```

### 7.3 Budget Decrease Conditions

A candidate should lose budget when it demonstrates:

```text
drift
noise
false recurrence
externalized cost
uncontained side effects
projection distortion
lineage break
repeated failure without repair
authority overreach
stake mismatch
uncertainty suppression
```

## 18. Example: Stop Sign as Authority / Reference

A stop sign is authoritative because it stabilizes consequence.

```text
symbolic handle:
STOP

projection:
red octagon in road-side visual field

target observers:
drivers, pedestrians, traffic systems

intended consequence:
slow / halt / yield behavioral transition

stake:
shared public safety

feedback history:
reduced collision risk and coordinated right-of-way

authority scope:
traffic context at a specific intersection
```

If the stop sign is placed thirty feet in the air:

```text
projection fidelity decreases
observer access decreases
response time degrades
context alignment fails
behavioral reliability drops
safety consequence weakens
authority budget collapses
```

The object did not lose its label.

It lost consequence capacity in context.
